Our methodology

How OfferSentry evaluates job-scam warning signs

OfferSentry is an educational first check. It looks for recognizable patterns that deserve a pause and independent verification; it does not determine whether a person or company is legitimate.

The process

Three steps, with the decision left to you

1

Recognize patterns

Our tools compare the text or public domain details you provide with plain-language warning signs commonly used in job scams.

2

Explain the concern

Each result names the pattern and explains why it matters, so a score never has to stand on its own.

3

Verify independently

Use an employer’s official website or a telephone number you find yourself—not contact details supplied by the recruiter.

Limits that matter

A result is not a verdict

No checker can prove that an offer is safe.

A scam can avoid a familiar phrase, and a legitimate message can contain wording that resembles a warning sign. Use OfferSentry as a starting point, not a substitute for your own judgment, the employer’s official information, or advice from a qualified professional.

  • OfferSentry does not investigate companies, recruiters, or job postings.
  • It does not make legal, financial, employment, or identity-theft determinations.
  • Its message checker runs in your browser and is designed not to upload or store the text you enter.
  • Public domain information can be useful context, but domain age and technical records do not prove legitimacy or fraud.

When a signal appears

Pause, preserve, and verify

Do not send money or sensitive information. Save the message, email address, phone number, payment instructions, and any job-posting link. Then use contact details from the employer’s official website to ask whether the role and recruiter are real. If you believe you encountered a scam, report it to the appropriate platform and consumer-protection agency.
