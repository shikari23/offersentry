Fake Check Job Scams and the “Equipment” Trap

A fake employer sends you a check, tells you to buy equipment, and directs you to send real money somewhere else. When the check is later discovered to be fraudulent, the money you sent is gone.

Published August 1, 2026 · Approximately 8 minutes

The quick answer

If an unfamiliar recruiter sends you a check and asks you to purchase equipment, send money to a vendor, buy gift cards, return an overpayment, or transfer part of the funds, stop. This is a major warning sign of a fake check job scam.

What is a fake check job scam?

A fake check job scam begins with someone pretending to represent a real or invented employer. The scammer may advertise a remote position, conduct a text-based interview, send an official-looking offer letter, and tell you that you have been hired.

After gaining your trust, the supposed employer sends you a check. They claim that the money is for a computer, printer, software, office furniture, training, or other work-from-home equipment.

The recruiter then directs you to purchase the equipment from a specific “approved vendor.” That vendor is usually controlled by the same scammer. In other cases, you may be told to send part of the money back, purchase gift cards, use a payment app, buy cryptocurrency, or make a wire transfer.

The check is fraudulent. The money you send to the scammer, however, is real.

Important warning

Money appearing in your bank account does not necessarily mean the check is valid. A bank can make funds available before it discovers that a check is fake.

How the equipment purchase scam works

1

The scammer contacts you

You receive an unexpected email, text, social media message, or invitation to interview for a remote position.

2

The interview feels unusually easy

The interview may happen entirely through text, Telegram, WhatsApp, Signal, Google Chat, or another messaging platform.

3

You receive a quick job offer

The scammer sends an offer letter with a company name, logo, employee names, salary, and other details copied from a legitimate business.

4

A check arrives

You are told to deposit the check using your banking app or at a branch. The amount may be several thousand dollars.

5

You are told to spend or transfer the money

The recruiter tells you to purchase equipment from a particular vendor, return an overpayment, or send money using a difficult-to- reverse payment method.

6

The check is reversed

Your bank eventually discovers that the check is fraudulent and removes the deposited amount. You may be responsible for money already withdrawn or transferred.

Why can a fake check look like it cleared?

Banks generally make at least some deposited funds available before the check has been fully verified. This helps customers access legitimate deposits without waiting through the entire processing period.

Scammers exploit the delay between funds becoming available and the bank discovering that the check is fraudulent. They pressure the victim to send money while the temporary balance is still visible.

A check can display as deposited, available, processed, or even cleared in an account interface and still later be returned. According to the Federal Trade Commission, discovering a fake check can take weeks.

The safest rule

Never use money from an unexpected check to pay a recruiter, purchase gift cards, send cryptocurrency, return an overpayment, or buy equipment from a vendor chosen by an unverified employer.

Warning signs of an equipment check scam

  • The employer sends a check before you have completed normal onboarding.
  • You are told to deposit the check through your mobile banking app.
  • The recruiter selects the equipment vendor and tells you where to send the money.
  • The check is for more money than you expected.
  • You are asked to return part of the payment.
  • You must buy gift cards, cryptocurrency, money orders, or prepaid cards.
  • The recruiter uses Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook, or another personal email account instead of the company’s domain.
  • The interview happens only through text or a messaging application.
  • You are hired unusually quickly without a normal video or telephone interview.
  • The recruiter asks for banking information, identification, or a Social Security number before you independently verify the employer.
  • You are pressured to act immediately or keep the process secret.

Example of a fake equipment message

Fictional example for education

“Congratulations. You have been selected for our remote administrative assistant position. The company will email you a check for $4,850 to purchase your laptop, printer, and required software. Deposit the check immediately and send confirmation to your hiring manager. You must purchase the equipment only from our approved vendor.”

A professional-looking message is not proof that the offer is real. Scammers can copy company logos, job descriptions, employee names, email signatures, and offer-letter templates.

The central warning sign is not the writing quality. It is the request that you deposit a check and then use or transfer the money.

Do legitimate employers pay for equipment?

Some real employers provide computers and other equipment to remote workers. However, they commonly ship company-owned equipment directly, use an established purchasing system, or reimburse approved expenses through documented company procedures.

An equipment benefit becomes suspicious when an unverified recruiter sends a check and instructs you to move the money to a particular person or vendor.

Before accepting equipment or payment instructions, contact the company using a telephone number or email address you found independently on its official website. Do not rely on contact information supplied by the recruiter.

What to do if you received a suspicious check

  1. Do not deposit it. Do not photograph it for mobile deposit, cash it, or attempt to use the funds.
  2. Stop communicating with the recruiter. Do not argue with them or follow additional instructions.
  3. Save the evidence. Keep the check, envelope, emails, text messages, usernames, phone numbers, offer letter, job posting, and payment instructions.
  4. Verify the company independently. Visit the company’s official website yourself and ask whether the recruiter and position are legitimate.
  5. Report the incident. Submit a report through ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

What if you already deposited the check?

  1. Do not spend or transfer the funds. Leave the deposited amount untouched.
  2. Contact your bank immediately. Ask for the fraud department and explain that you may have deposited a check connected to a job scam.
  3. Follow the bank’s instructions. Provide accurate information about how you received the check and any communications with the sender.
  4. Continue monitoring your account. Watch for returned-check notices, unexpected transactions, or contact from the scammer.

What if you already sent money?

Act immediately. Contact the bank, payment application, wire-transfer company, gift-card issuer, or cryptocurrency platform used for the transaction. Tell them that the payment was connected to fraud and ask whether it can be stopped or reversed.

Recovery is not guaranteed, but acting quickly provides the best chance of limiting the loss.

If you shared personal information, visit IdentityTheft.gov for a personalized recovery plan. Change exposed passwords and contact the appropriate financial institutions.

How to verify a job before accepting it

  1. Search for the company’s official website without using the recruiter’s link.
  2. Confirm that the same position appears on the official careers page.
  3. Compare the recruiter’s email domain with the company’s real domain.
  4. Call the company using a number found independently on its website.
  5. Search the company and recruiter names alongside words such as “scam,” “complaint,” or “fake job.”
  6. Never pay a fee, buy gift cards, or transfer money to receive a job.

Frequently asked questions

Can a fake check appear to clear?

Yes. Your bank may make deposited funds available before discovering that the check is fraudulent. A visible balance does not prove that the check is valid.

Do legitimate employers send checks for equipment?

A legitimate employer may provide equipment or reimburse approved expenses. However, you should not deposit a check and then send money to a vendor selected by an unverified recruiter.

Can a cashier’s check be fake?

Yes. Cashier’s checks, business checks, certified checks, and money orders can be counterfeited or altered.

What if the recruiter is using a real company’s name?

Scammers frequently impersonate legitimate companies. Contact the company through its official website and confirm the recruiter’s identity independently.

Should I return the check to the recruiter?

Do not follow further instructions from the sender. Preserve the check as evidence and ask your bank or the appropriate authorities how it should be handled.
