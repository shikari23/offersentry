Recruiter verification guide

How to Verify a Recruiter Is Legitimate

Before replying, accepting an interview, or sharing personal information, use these 10 checks to independently confirm the recruiter, company, and position.

Published August 1, 2026 · Approximately 9 minutes

The quick answer

Do not verify a recruiter using only information the recruiter provided. Find the company’s official website yourself, confirm the position on its careers page, compare the email domains, and contact the company through an independently located telephone number or email address.

Why recruiter verification matters

Job scammers frequently impersonate real companies and real employees. They can copy names, photographs, logos, job descriptions, email signatures, offer letters, and information from public professional profiles.

Finding the company online is therefore not enough. The important question is whether the person contacting you is actually authorized to recruit for that company and position.

Verification should happen outside the original conversation. A telephone number, website, profile, or email address supplied by the recruiter could be part of the impersonation.

Verification must be independent

Do not call a number from the recruiter’s email signature to verify that same recruiter. Find the company’s official contact information through a separate search.

1

Confirm that the job exists

Open a new browser tab and search for the company yourself. Navigate to the careers section of the company’s official website and look for the same position.

Compare the job title, location, department, responsibilities, and application deadline. A scammer may copy an expired listing or alter a legitimate description.

The absence of a public listing does not automatically prove fraud. Some employers use private searches or staffing agencies. However, the company should still be able to confirm that the opening exists.

2

Inspect the complete email address

Do not rely on the displayed sender name. An email can display “Company Recruiting Team” while the actual address belongs to an unrelated account.

Expand the sender details and inspect everything after the @ symbol.

Example

Expected: recruiter@company.com Concerning: company.hiring.team@gmail.com

The words before the @ symbol do not determine which organization owns the account. In the second example, the email is still a Gmail account.

3

Compare the email and website domains

Compare the recruiter’s email domain with the company’s official website domain character by character.

Lookalike examples

Official: northstarcompany.com Lookalike: northstar-company.com Lookalike: northstarcompanny.com Lookalike: northstarcompany-careers.com

Added words, hyphens, replaced letters, repeated letters, and different domain endings can be signs of impersonation.

4

Do not treat a matching domain as proof

A matching company domain is encouraging, but it is not a guarantee. Email sender information can be spoofed, and legitimate email accounts can sometimes be compromised.

Confirm the recruiter through a separate communication channel. Call the company’s main telephone number or contact its human-resources department using information found on the official website.

Use two independent signals

A company-domain email plus confirmation through the company’s official switchboard is stronger evidence than either one alone.

5

Research the domain’s registration

Domain-registration information can help identify a website created recently or registered separately from the real company domain.

Use the ICANN Registration Data Lookup Tool to research the domain. ICANN uses the Registration Data Access Protocol, commonly called RDAP.

Compare the domain’s creation date with the company’s history. A website claiming to represent a decades-old organization but registered only days ago deserves serious scrutiny.

Registration details may be privacy-protected or unavailable. A new domain is not automatically fraudulent, and an old domain is not automatically trustworthy. Treat registration data as one signal, not a final verdict.

6

Verify the recruiter’s professional identity

Search the recruiter’s name along with the company name. Look for consistent employment history, professional activity, and contact information.

A professional profile alone does not prove legitimacy. Scammers can create fake profiles or impersonate a real employee whose information is publicly available.

If you find a real employee with the same name, do not assume the person contacting you controls that profile. Contact the company or the employee through a separate verified channel.

7

Evaluate the interview process

Consider whether the process matches the position and employer. A rushed text-only interview for a professional role deserves additional verification.

Warning signs include immediate hiring, no meaningful questions, no discussion of your experience, refusal to conduct a telephone or video conversation, and pressure to complete onboarding immediately.

A long interview does not prove legitimacy either. Some scammers invest significant time to make their impersonation convincing.

8

Refuse every payment request

You should not have to pay a recruiter or employer to receive a job. Do not pay application fees, training costs, certification fees, background-check fees, software charges, or equipment vendors selected by an unverified recruiter.

Never deposit a check and then send part of the money elsewhere. Do not purchase gift cards, cryptocurrency, money orders, or prepaid cards for an employer.

9

Protect sensitive information

Legitimate employers need certain personal and financial information after hiring. The timing and method matter.

Be cautious if a recruiter requests a Social Security number, banking details, driver’s license, passport, password, verification code, or photograph of identification before a verified interview and formal hiring process.

Ask why the information is needed, how it will be protected, and whether it can be submitted through an official secure employee portal.

10

Contact the company directly

This is the strongest practical check. Find the company’s official website independently and contact its human-resources department, recruiting team, or main switchboard.

Provide the recruiter’s name, email address, job title, and any reference number. Ask whether the person is authorized to recruit for that position.

Do not use the recruiter’s links or telephone numbers for this step. The verification channel must be separate from the original contact.

Legitimate versus suspicious recruiter behavior

More consistent with legitimate hiring Requires additional verification
The job appears on the official company careers page. The position cannot be found or confirmed.
The recruiter uses a matching company email domain. The recruiter uses a personal or lookalike domain.
The company confirms the recruiter independently. Every verification method comes from the recruiter.
The interview includes relevant questions and real discussion. You are hired immediately through text messages.
Equipment is shipped or handled through documented procedures. You receive a check and must pay an approved vendor.
Sensitive onboarding occurs after verified hiring. Banking or identity information is requested before an interview.

Recruiter verification checklist

  • I found the company website independently.
  • I confirmed that the position exists.
  • I inspected the recruiter’s complete email address.
  • I compared the email and company domains.
  • I checked for misspellings and extra words in the domain.
  • I researched the domain’s registration information.
  • I investigated the recruiter’s professional identity.
  • The interview process makes sense for the position.
  • I have not been asked to pay or transfer money.
  • The company confirmed the recruiter through a separate contact method.

Frequently asked questions

How can I verify that a recruiter is legitimate?

Find the company’s official website yourself, confirm the position, compare the recruiter’s email domain, and contact the company through an independently located telephone number or email address.

Does a company email prove that the recruiter is real?

No. A matching domain is encouraging, but sender information can be spoofed and accounts can be compromised. Confirm the recruiter through another company contact method.

Can a legitimate recruiter use Gmail?

Independent recruiters and small businesses may use personal email services. However, you should perform additional verification and confirm their relationship with the employer before sharing information.

Is a professional profile enough to verify a recruiter?

No. Scammers can copy real employee information or create fake profiles. Confirm the person through the company’s official contact information.

When should an employer request banking information?

Banking information may be needed for payroll after legitimate hiring. It should not be the focus of an initial recruiter conversation or requested before the employer and position have been independently verified.
