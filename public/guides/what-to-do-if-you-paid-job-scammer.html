Job-scam recovery guide

What to Do If You Paid a Job Scammer

Act immediately, contact the provider used to send the money, preserve the evidence, report the fake job, and watch for recovery scams.

Written and reviewed by Nehal Patel · Published August 6, 2026 · Approximately 10 minutes

The quick answer

Contact the company used to send the payment immediately. Use the official application, website, card, receipt, or account statement—not contact information supplied by the recruiter. Explain that the payment was connected to a fake-job scam and ask whether it can be stopped, recalled, reversed, refunded, or investigated.

Do not send another payment

Scammers commonly claim that another fee, deposit, tax, verification payment, or account upgrade is required to release earnings or return lost money. Sending more money usually increases the loss.

Act according to how you paid

Recovery options differ by payment method. Acting quickly may improve the chance of stopping a pending transaction, but no recovery method is guaranteed.

Credit card or debit card

Contact the card issuer using the number printed on the card or shown in its official application. Explain that the charge was connected to fraud and ask whether the transaction can be reversed or disputed.

If card information was exposed, ask whether the card should be locked or replaced. Review the account for unfamiliar activity.

Bank transfer or withdrawal

Contact the bank or credit union’s fraud department immediately. Explain whether you initiated the transfer after being deceived or whether someone moved money without your permission.

Ask whether a pending transfer can be stopped or a completed transfer can be recalled. Report unauthorized activity promptly.

Zelle, Cash App, Venmo, PayPal, or another payment app

Report the payment through the application’s official support or fraud process. Contact the linked bank or card issuer as well.

Tell each provider exactly what happened. Do not describe a payment you personally approved as unauthorized; payments induced by deception and unauthorized account access may be handled differently.

Wire transfer

Contact the bank or wire-transfer company immediately. Provide the transfer number and tell the company the transfer was connected to fraud. Ask whether it can be stopped or recalled.

Preserve the receipt, destination information, transfer number, and all instructions from the fake recruiter.

Gift card

Contact the company that issued the gift card. Tell it the card was used in a scam and ask whether any balance can be frozen or refunded.

Keep the physical card, packaging, purchase receipt, card number, and any photographs or messages showing where the code was sent.

Cryptocurrency

Contact the cryptocurrency exchange or platform used to send the transaction. Report the receiving wallet address and ask whether the account or transaction can be investigated.

Cryptocurrency transactions are often difficult or impossible to reverse. Preserve the wallet address, transaction identifier, amount, exchange account, receipts, and communications.

Cash, cashier’s check, or money order

Contact the bank, issuer, postal service, delivery carrier, or money order company involved. If an envelope or shipment is still moving, ask whether delivery can be stopped.

Keep copies of receipts, serial numbers, tracking information, addresses, and delivery instructions.

A check from the “employer”

If you deposited a recruiter’s check and then sent money elsewhere, contact your bank immediately. Do not spend any remaining funds from the check.

A deposited check can appear available before the bank discovers it is fraudulent and removes the funds.

Recovery steps after the payment

1

Stop communicating and paying

Do not send another fee, deposit, tax, refund, gift card, cryptocurrency payment, or verification code. Do not follow instructions to move money into a different account for “protection.”

2

Contact the payment provider immediately

Use an independently verified telephone number, application, website, card, statement, or receipt. Ask whether the payment is pending and whether any stop, cancellation, recall, reversal, refund, or dispute process is available.

3

Describe the transaction accurately

Tell the provider whether you approved the payment after being deceived or whether someone accessed your account and made a transaction without permission. These situations may be investigated under different rules.

4

Preserve the evidence

Save recruiter messages, emails, telephone numbers, usernames, job listings, offer letters, receipts, payment confirmations, wallet addresses, transaction identifiers, bank records, gift cards, and delivery information.

5

Secure affected accounts

Change exposed passwords using official websites or applications. Secure the email account connected to financial accounts, review recovery information, enable multifactor authentication, and report unfamiliar activity.

6

Report the scam

Report the fake job to the platform where it appeared, the real company being impersonated, the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov , and the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center when appropriate.

Authorized payment versus unauthorized activity

A payment you personally approved after believing a scammer’s story is different from a transaction made by someone who accessed your account without permission.

Be accurate when speaking with the bank or payment provider. Explain that a fake recruiter deceived you, describe exactly how the payment occurred, and provide the evidence. Ask which recovery or complaint process applies.

Do not assume that an authorized payment cannot be reported. Contact the provider immediately, but understand that reimbursement is not guaranteed.

Watch for refund and recovery scams

After losing money, you may receive calls or messages from people claiming to be investigators, government employees, attorneys, hackers, bank representatives, or recovery specialists.

Guaranteed recovery is a warning sign

Do not pay an upfront fee or provide more financial information to someone promising to recover the money. Verify every organization independently using contact information you find yourself.

Payment-recovery checklist

  • I stopped communicating with the fake recruiter.
  • I did not send another payment or verification code.
  • I contacted the payment provider through an official channel.
  • I accurately explained whether I authorized the payment.
  • I asked about stopping, recalling, reversing, or disputing it.
  • I saved receipts, messages, account details, and transaction IDs.
  • I secured affected financial and email accounts.
  • I reported the fake job and impersonated company.
  • I submitted reports to the appropriate authorities.
  • I will not pay anyone promising guaranteed recovery.

Frequently asked questions

Can I recover money paid to a fake recruiter?

Recovery depends on the payment method, timing, and circumstances. Contact the provider immediately and ask what options remain. Recovery is never guaranteed.

What if I paid through Zelle or another payment app?

Report the payment through the application’s official support process and contact the linked bank or card issuer. Explain truthfully whether you approved the payment or someone accessed your account.

What if I paid using gift cards?

Contact the gift-card issuer immediately. Keep the cards and receipts, report that they were used in a scam, and ask whether any remaining balance can be frozen or refunded.

Can cryptocurrency be reversed?

Cryptocurrency transfers are often difficult or impossible to reverse. Contact the exchange immediately and preserve the receiving address, transaction identifier, receipts, and communications.

Should I hire someone to recover the money?

Be extremely cautious. Do not pay upfront fees or trust anyone promising guaranteed recovery. Verify organizations independently.
